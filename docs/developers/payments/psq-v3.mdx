# Payment Status Query v3

The status query answers one question: **what is the state of this payment, and what happened on every try?** You send a `session_id` or an `order_no`, and Ottu answers with the **payment object**: the payment's own `status`, its money `totals`, and **every attempt** made on it, oldest first, refunds and captures included.

Your [webhook](/developers/webhooks) tells you when something happens. Use the status query when a webhook is missing, when you reconcile, and after a [`502` or `504` from Operations v3](/developers/operations-v3#after-504).

Every call on this page targets <code>{OTTU_CONNECT_BASE_URL}</code>: swap in your own merchant domain when you integrate. Authentication is the same as for every Ottu API: see [Authentication](/developers/getting-started/authentication).

## When to Use

- A webhook did not arrive, and you need the payment's current state.
- An operation answered `504 gateway_timeout` or `502 gateway_error`, and you must know whether it happened before you retry with a new key.
- You reconcile your records with Ottu: `totals` and `attempts` rebuild the whole history of a payment. See [Reconciliation](/developers/webhooks#missed-webhooks).

## Setup

- **A payment.** Its `session_id` (always present) or its `order_no`, from the [Checkout API](/developers/payments/checkout-api) or the dashboard.
- **An API key or user with the `payment.inquiry` permission.** See [Authentication](/developers/getting-started/authentication).

## Guide

### Workflow {#workflow}

1. **You send `session_id` or `order_no`.** The identifier of a refund or a capture also works: Ottu answers with the payment it belongs to.
2. **Ottu checks with the gateway when the payment is still open.** For a payment that is `pending`, `attempted`, `failed` or `expired`, Ottu first asks the gateways that support a status check. For `paid` and `authorized`, it answers straight away.
3. **Ottu answers with the payment object.**

<PsqWorkflowSequence />

### Step-by-Step {#step-by-step}

<Beside>

1. **Send the payment's identifier.** `session_id` is preferred. Use `order_no` when you do not have it.
2. **Read `status`.** It is the payment's own [state](/developers/reference/payment-states).
3. **Read `totals`.** `paid` minus `refunded` and `voided` is what you kept. `refund_pending` is money still on its way back.
4. **Walk `attempts`** when you need the details: every try and every operation, oldest first.

The answer is [the payment object](#payment-object).

### Send the result to your webhook {#notify}

Two optional fields make Ottu send the result to a webhook as well:

- `notify_webhook_url`: `true` sends it to the payment's webhook URL.
- `webhook_url`: sends that webhook to this address instead.

## API Reference

`POST /b/pbl/v3/inquiry/` with a JSON body. The trailing slash is optional.

**Returns** [the payment object](#payment-object).

### Request fields {#request}

#### `session_id` {#field-session_id}

String, at most 128 characters. The payment. Send `session_id` or `order_no`.

#### `order_no` {#field-order_no}

String, at most 128 characters. The payment, when you have no `session_id`.

#### `notify_webhook_url` {#field-notify_webhook_url}

Boolean, `false` by default. Also send the result to the payment's webhook URL.

#### `webhook_url` {#field-webhook_url}

URL. Send that webhook here instead.

### The payment object {#payment-object}

<Beside>

The payment itself, with the same keys as the [`data` of a payment event](/developers/webhooks#data-keys). One thing differs: the keys of a single attempt (`operation`, `processed_at`, `result`, `message`, `channel`, `form_of_payment`, `card`, `connector`) are not at the root. A payment can have many attempts, so they are in `attempts`.

Every key is always present. An empty value is `null`, an empty list is `[]`, and an empty object keeps its keys with `null` values.

#### `session_id`, `order_no` {#object-identifiers}

Your identifiers for the payment.

#### `payment_type`, `language`, `amount`, `currency` {#object-as-created}

The payment as it was created.

#### `status` {#object-status}

The payment's own [state](/developers/reference/payment-states).

#### `status_changed_at` {#object-status-changed-at}

When the payment reached that status, or `null`.

#### `totals` {#object-totals}

`paid`, `settled`, `refunded`, `refund_pending`, `voided` and `remaining`, in the payment's currency.

#### `customer`, `billing_address`, `shipping_address`, `agreement`, `wallets`, `extra`, `created_by` {#object-shared}

The same as in a [payment event](/developers/webhooks#data-keys).

#### `created_at`, `updated_at` {#object-dates}

ISO 8601, UTC.

The example is a payment that was declined once, paid, then refunded twice. It is shortened: a real answer carries every key listed here.

#### `attempts` {#attempts}

Every attempt made on the payment, **oldest first**: the customer's tries, failed ones included, and the captures, voids and refunds run later. Each attempt has:

- `operation`: `purchase` or `authorize` for a customer's try; `capture`, `refund` or `void` for an operation.
- `processed_at`: when the attempt reached its result.
- `result`: `pending`, `success`, `failed`, `canceled`, `error` or `cod`.
- `message`: the result text.
- `channel`, `form_of_payment`: how the customer paid. `null` on operations.
- `card`: the card used.
- `connector`: everything at the gateway: `code`, `identifier`, `processor`, `reference_number`, `currency`, `amount`, `exchange_rate`, `fee`, `total`, [`params`](/developers/webhooks/pg-params) and `responses`.

What to know about the list:

- **A pending refund** is an attempt with `operation` `refund` and `result` `pending`, and it counts in `totals.refund_pending`. If the gateway rejects it, the same attempt becomes `result` `failed` and leaves the totals.
- **Not listed:** attempts Ottu records for its own bookkeeping where no gateway ran, such as a cash on delivery acknowledgement.
- **Not listed:** the wallet part of a split payment. It is in `wallets`.
- **A removed gateway:** an attempt whose payment gateway was later removed is still listed, with an empty `connector`.

### Answers {#responses}

`200` carries the payment object. Every other status carries an error in the [error format](/errors#format):

- **`400`** No identifier, an invalid field, a payment in a state the status query does not apply to (for example `canceled`), or a payment with no attempts yet, unless it has expired (an expired payment with no attempts answers `200` with `status` `expired` and an empty `attempts` list). These errors have `attr` `null`.
- **`401`** [`not_authenticated`](/errors#not_authenticated).
- **`403`** [`permission_denied`](/errors#permission_denied): the `payment.inquiry` permission is missing, or the [IP allowlist](/developers/operations-v3#rules-every-call-follows) refused the call.
- **`404`** [`payment_not_found`](/errors#payment_not_found): an unknown identifier, or a payment of another unit when your API user is limited to units.
- **`500`** [`server_error`](/errors#server_error).

## Best Practices

- **Let webhooks tell you.** Ask the status query when a webhook is missing, or after a `502` or `504`.
- **Reconcile on `totals`.** `paid` minus `refunded` and `voided` is what you kept.
- **Match refunds on `connector.reference_number`**, the id Ottu sent to the gateway. It is the line in the gateway's settlement report.
- **Ignore unknown keys, and unknown `operation` and `result` values.** New ones can be added.

## FAQ

<FAQ>
  <FAQItem question="How do I find out if my refund happened after a 504?">
    Ask for the payment and look for an entry in `attempts` with `operation` `refund`, then check `totals.refunded` and `totals.refund_pending`. If the refund is there, do not send it again. If it is not, retry with a **new** `Idempotency-Key`. See [After a 502 or 504](/developers/operations-v3#after-504).
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Why is there no result at the root of the answer?">
    A payment can have many attempts, so there is no single current one. Read the payment's `status`, and walk `attempts` for the details.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Is the answer the same as a webhook event?">
    The payment keys are the same. An event describes one moment, with one attempt's keys beside the payment's. This answer describes the whole payment and lists every attempt.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Does it ask the gateway?">
    Yes, for a payment that is `pending`, `attempted`, `failed` or `expired`, on the gateways that support a status check. A `paid` or `authorized` payment is answered straight away.
  </FAQItem>
</FAQ>
