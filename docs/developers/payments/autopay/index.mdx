# AutoPay

AutoPay runs your subscriptions for you. You create a subscription with one [Checkout API](../checkout-api) call, and your customer pays the first charge. From then on Ottu charges their saved card on every billing date, retries a charge that fails, emails the customer, and gives them a page where they manage the subscription themselves. You build no scheduler, no retry loop and no [dunning](/glossary/#term-dunning) emails.

:::tip See what your customers see
The [AutoPay business page](/business/autopay/) walks through the whole experience from the customer's side: the emails, the self-service page, and what happens when a payment fails.
:::

Everything on this page uses the [Ottu API key](/developers/getting-started/authentication) you already have. There is no separate credential, and no AutoPay screen in the dashboard yet: you manage subscriptions through the API.

:::note Base URL
Every API call in this guide targets <code>{OTTU_CONNECT_BASE_URL}</code>. Swap in your own merchant domain when you integrate.
:::

:::tip Boost Your Integration
Ottu offers SDKs and tools to speed up your integration. See [Getting Started](../../getting-started/#boost-your-integration) for all available options.
:::

## When to Use

- **Subscriptions and SaaS billing** — monthly or yearly plans where you don't want to build and operate a billing scheduler.
- **You want Ottu to handle dunning** — failed-charge retries, the past-due state, and recovery emails happen automatically.
- **You want a customer self-service surface** without building one — a hosted page for viewing status, paying an outstanding balance, updating the card, or canceling.
- **You have no plan catalog to manage** — there's no "create a plan" step; pricing and scheduling are just fields on your checkout call.

:::tip Choosing between AutoPay and Auto-Debit
Use AutoPay when you want Ottu to own the whole subscription lifecycle after one checkout call. If you'd rather run the billing schedule and retry logic yourself and just charge a saved card on demand, use [Recurring Payments & Auto-Debit](/developers/cards-and-tokens/recurring-payments) instead — it's the closer fit for installments, on-demand billing, and cases where your own system needs to decide exactly when a charge happens.
:::

## Guide

### Workflow

<AutoPayFlowDiagram />

1. **You create the subscription.** One call to the Checkout API, with the price and how often to charge. The subscription exists as soon as the call returns, and you get its id straight back.
2. **Your customer pays the first charge** on the Ottu checkout, the same way they pay any other order. Their card is saved for the renewals.
3. **Ottu tells you the payment went through**, and the subscription goes live a few minutes later.
4. **Ottu takes over.** It charges the saved card on each billing date, retries a charge that fails, and emails the customer before each charge and whenever something goes wrong.
5. **Your customer manages it themselves**, on a page you link them to: they see their history, change their card, pay what they owe, or cancel.

### Use Cases

#### Subscription states {#subscription-states}

A subscription waits in `pending_setup` until its first payment is confirmed. It then becomes `active`, or `trialing` if the first charge was zero. A subscription whose renewal could not be collected after every retry is `past_due`. It ends as `canceled`, when you or the customer cancel, or `expired`, when it reaches its end date. If the first payment never completes, it is `setup_failed`, and a new attempt at the first payment moves it back to `pending_setup`. See [Subscription lifecycle](/business/autopay/#subscription-lifecycle).

#### Billing cycles {#billing-cycles}

Each charge belongs to a billing cycle, and the next cycle is created only when the current one is paid. A cycle charges the amount it was created with, and a subscription billed on the 31st is charged on the last day of shorter months. See [Billing cycles](/business/autopay/#billing-cycles).

#### Failed charges {#retries-and-dunning}

When a renewal fails, Ottu tries again an hour later, and keeps trying an hour apart: three more times by default, which you can change per subscription. The customer is emailed after a failed attempt. When every attempt has failed, the subscription becomes `past_due` and the customer gets an email with a link to pay. **A past-due subscription is never canceled automatically**: it stays past due until the customer pays or you cancel it. See [Retries and dunning](/business/autopay/#retries-and-dunning).

#### Notifications {#notifications}

Ottu sends three emails, in English and Arabic: a reminder before each charge, a notice when a charge fails, and a final notice with a payment link when every retry has failed. Monthly plans get one reminder, two days before the charge; yearly plans get three, at 30, 10 and 3 days. You can turn each email off per subscription. See [Notifications](/business/autopay/#notifications).

#### The customer's self-service page {#customer-self-service-page}

Each subscription has its own link, which you send to the customer. Whoever holds the link can manage the subscription, so treat it like a password. A customer who cancels from the page keeps the service until the end of the period. See [The customer self-service page](/business/autopay/#customer-self-service-page).

## API Reference

You create a subscription with the Checkout API, and manage it with six subscription endpoints on the same host and the same API key. Each tab is the interactive reference for one call, with every field described and an example for every response.

All six subscription endpoints share three failure statuses: `423` while the installation is paused, `503` when AutoPay is unavailable, and `504` when it timed out. Retry `503` and `504` with backoff. A `504` does not mean nothing happened: retry a cancel with the same `Idempotency-Key`, and read the subscription before retrying anything else.

## Best Practices

- **Store `extra.autopay.subscription_id`** from the create response. It is the only handle on the subscription, and the request's `autopay` block is not echoed back.
- **Describe the same schedule twice.** The `agreement` is what the card network sees and the `autopay` block is what AutoPay bills on, so give them the same frequency.
- **Send an `Idempotency-Key` (UUID v4) on Cancel and Regenerate.** On Cancel, retrying with the same key after a timeout is safe. On Regenerate it is not: the same key still mints a new link and revokes the old one.
- **Read the link, don't regenerate it.** To send the customer their link again, use Page link. Regenerating revokes the link they already have, and Ottu does not tell them.
- **Watch for past-due subscriptions.** Ottu never cancels them, so build your own follow-up if your business needs one.
- **Set `card_acceptance_criteria.min_expiry_time` yourself** if you want to refuse cards that expire soon. Auto-debit defaults it to 30 days; AutoPay does not.
- **Match `notification_preferences` keys exactly.** Connect passes the object through unchecked, so a misspelled key is ignored and that email stays on.

## FAQ

<FAQ>
  <FAQItem question="How do I know when the first payment is done?">
    From the payment webhook, the same one you get for any Checkout API payment if you set `webhook_url`. It reports `payment_type: "auto_pay"` and carries the subscription's id in `extra.autopay.subscription_id`, inside the [signed fields](/developers/webhooks/verify-signatures). That tells you the first charge succeeded. The subscription itself moves from `pending_setup` to `active` shortly after, once AutoPay confirms the payment on its own schedule, so allow a few minutes. Use [Retrieve](#api-reference) as the fallback when a webhook does not arrive, or to confirm the subscription is live.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="How do I test a failed renewal?">
    On the sandbox, pay the first charge with one of the [MIT-failure test cards](/developers/payments/ottu-sandbox#test-cards): `411111111111114` (declined by the issuer), `411111111111115` (expired) or `411111111111116` (insufficient funds). The first payment succeeds and the card is saved, but every renewal charged to it is declined, so the subscription goes through the retries and ends `past_due`. Renewals run on the billing date.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="What happens when the saved card expires?">
    The renewal is declined like any failed charge: Ottu retries it, emails the customer, and marks the subscription `past_due` when the retries run out. The customer adds a new card and pays on their self-service page, and the subscription becomes `active` again. Ottu does not check the expiry date before charging.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Can I change the amount of a running subscription?">
    No. There is no endpoint that edits a subscription. To change the price, cancel the subscription and create a new one. Each billing cycle charges the amount it was created with.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Where do I see AutoPay charges in the dashboard?">
    Every charge is an ordinary Ottu payment, so it appears with your other payments. The first charge has payment type `auto_pay`; each renewal is an auto-debit payment that AutoPay makes with the saved card. There is no subscription screen in the dashboard yet: list and inspect subscriptions through the [API](#api-reference).
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="What happens if I leave out customer_email?">
    The checkout call fails with `400 {"autopay": ["AutoPayClient rejected request (422)"]}`. AutoPay needs the email to reach the customer. The message reads like an agreement problem, but a missing `customer_email` is the usual cause.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Why does the Checkout SDK show payment_type auto_debit?">
    `auto_pay` only means something to Ottu, which runs the subscription. The gateway and the SDK see an ordinary auto-debit payment, so the SDK's session data shows `auto_debit`. Your API response and your webhook still say `auto_pay`.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="A card saved before the agreement fix keeps failing. Why?">
    An earlier defect saved cards from `auto_pay` first payments without their agreement, and renewals against such a card are rejected with "No Cardholder Initiated Transaction (CIT) found". Those cards are not repaired automatically. Have the customer complete a new first payment so the card is saved with its agreement.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="How do I check that AutoPay is enabled for my account?">
    `GET /b/api/v1/config/` includes `autopay_api_url` when AutoPay is enabled, and leaves the key out entirely when it is not. Test for the key, not its value.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Does Ottu ever cancel a past-due subscription?">
    No. It stays past due until the customer pays or you cancel it.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Can a customer cancel immediately?">
    No. A customer's cancellation from the self-service page takes effect at the end of the current period, and they can undo it until then. Only you can cancel immediately, with `mode: "immediate"`.
  </FAQItem>
</FAQ>
